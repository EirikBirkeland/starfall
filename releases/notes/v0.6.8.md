The Eclipse's last phase lifts an arm: three fans of light are lit, and then a blade comes down each one.
- **A hazard that moves has to show the whole of the way it moves.** The Eclipse's phase 3 lights three
  wedges out of its own facing - from the bearing a blade is held at to the bearing it dies at, at the
  length it reaches and the width it hurts at - and a second later the blades come round. What you were
  shown is the harm, edge for edge: there is one number for how wide a blade is painted and how wide it
  takes a hull off you, and one pair for how far it reaches either way. Three fans sweep 2.7 radians of
  the sky's 6.28, so more than half the room is never touched; what the fight charges for finding it is
  the drag, which leans you inwards where the blades are slower.
- **A blade is loose, not attached.** Once let go it belongs to the world and not to the fight: it turns
  on its own, it follows the body that made it while that body stands, and it finishes its sweep over the
  wreck of a boss that went down halfway through. It costs a hull and one, and it names the fight that
  loosed it, so the end-of-run tally can tell you whose arm you walked into.
- **The dark cannot eat it.** Phase 3 is also the darkest room in the game, and a fan the night had
  swallowed would not be a thing to dodge but a wall to be standing inside when it arrived. The veil is
  cut with the same question the frame is painted with, so the light and the holes in the dark are the
  same wedge and the same band, measured from the same numbers the harm is paid out at.
- **The simulated player reads it now.** It slides out of the wedge if there is time to get round to an
  edge, and dives inside the rim the blades start from - the one place in the room a blade can never
  reach - if there is not. Four new claims in `test/dodge.test.mjs` say so, and go red if it stops
  knowing.
- **29 new tests, and the harness got two things out of it.** Painting one thing on a canvas of its own
  now says *where* it drew as well as what it cost, so a test can prove a blade is drawn at exactly twice
  the width that hurts you instead of hoping nothing else in the frame drew a line that wide. And the
  night's own sheet logs the paths it was cut with, which is the only way to claim a hole was cut in the
  dark and not merely that something happened.
