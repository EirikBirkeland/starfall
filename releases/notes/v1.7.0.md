The settings panel has a thumb shape: a phone visitor can open it, change it, and get out again.
- **`SETTINGS` is the fourth chip on the title rail.** It was left off the first three because a chip that
  opens a page a finger can neither use nor leave is a trap and not a control. The panel it opens now answers
  a tap, so the bar is met from the other side.
- **A tap on a row moves that row to its next value.** A row is pressed by the same arithmetic as a chip — the
  box the browser laid out, not one recomputed in JavaScript — and it answers with its own next step: a picture
  row walks its table and comes back round to its first, a slider goes past the top to its bottom, the mute
  flips, and the two switches a thumb run turns on go off. That last part is the reason a tap is not simply
  “the right arrow”: most of these rows ship at the top of their travel, where the right arrow answers nothing
  at all. The arrow keys keep their no-wrapping rule, unchanged.
- **`CLOSE`, standing on the panel.** It is the `O` key — the same toggle, so a panel shut from a chip over a
  held run puts the pause back up exactly as the key does. A visit with a keyboard is not shown one, because it
  has the letter.
- **The panel says what this hand can do.** A thumb reads `TAP A ROW TO CHANGE IT` and is no longer told about
  `O`, `Esc`, `M` and `[ ] - =`; a visit with a keyboard reads precisely the words the page ships.
- **The controls rack is not offered to a visit that has never pressed a key.** Every verb behind that door is a
  key press, so the row that opens it is painted only once a key has actually been pressed — which also keeps a
  finger out of a rack it could enter and could not leave.
