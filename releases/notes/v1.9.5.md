The sniper gets looked at on its own account for once: its eye fills four fifths of the circle that hurts you, it sits a step deeper in its red, and the three pairs the crowd owed it in colour are paid.
- **The eye covers 84% of its own hurtbox now**, up from 74% and from 53% before that — same two corners
  standing 1.5r fore and aft, flanks now 0.8r off the axis instead of 0.7. It reaches no further past the
  circle a bullet is stopped by than it did, and the pupil the frame lights inside it has room to sit in.
  It stops there on purpose: at 1.7 it would have covered 88% and come within 3.8 px of a bomber's studs,
  which spends a twelfth pair of bodies drawn as one shape — and past that an almond is a disc with two
  thorns in it, and what makes this body an eye is that it comes to a point at both ends.
- **The body went a step deeper in lightness** (#d81e5b -> #bd1250), hue and chroma left exactly where they
  were. That is the cheapest step the palette has, because this is the one body in the arena that never
  moves and so is not also being told apart by how it walks. Three bodies had landed within a step of it —
  the darter at 0.45 of a step, the swarmling at 0.76, the bomber at 0.92 — and they are 1.02, 1.33 and
  1.49 now, which takes the first two off the palette report's crowded list and `bomber / sniper` off its
  thin-outline one. Being the darkest red in the crowd is the kind's own argument anyway: the socket is
  meant to be dark so that the white of its pupil and of its beam are the loudest light on the glass.
- **The interior wash came up while the outline went down** (0.20 -> 0.26 of an opacity), or the body would
  have been a hoop with a hollow middle. Measured against the sky it clears its bar by 0.23 of a step now
  instead of by 0.08, which was the tightest number in that audit.
- **What it cost, said out loud**: the eye against the arena's own wall came from 1.32 of a step apart to
  0.75, against a promise of 0.4 — a wall is painted at very nearly the lightness the body now has, and for
  an eye with no hue a wall and a body have nothing else. It is the sixth-tightest of the table's 84
  promises, and the report prints it by name every time anybody runs it.
- **One more thing is measured instead of trusted**: `test/palette-read.test.mjs` now holds the sentence the
  palette has always leaned on in a comment — that the body which never moves wears the deepest colour the
  crowd has — and holds that it is still a red and not something else that happens to be dark. Suite 2431,
  `palette-check --strict` and `contrast-check --strict` clean, and the reference run is byte for byte what
  it was: what a body looks like is not what it does.
